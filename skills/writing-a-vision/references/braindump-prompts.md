# Brain-dump prompts — for the vision

Hand this to the project owner and let them talk. This is the *input*: it feeds the whole vision. The agent's job is only to organize what comes out, then ask about gaps.

**Tell them:** Just talk. Speech-to-text is fine. Don't worry about order, grammar, or saying it cleanly — react to the prompts and say what you're actually thinking. Skip any that don't fit. Ramble as long as you want; I'll read it and ask follow-ups only where something's missing.

---

**Why does this project exist?**
What's the problem? What's broken, slow, expensive, or impossible without it? Who feels that pain, and when? If this project disappeared tomorrow, what would go wrong?

**What is it, in plain words?**
If you described it to a friend who isn't technical, what would you say it is and does? Don't reach for jargon — just say what it is.

**Walk me through it working.**
Picture it finished and working well. What does someone do with it? What goes in, what happens, what comes out? What's the moment that tells you "yes, this is doing its job"?

**Who uses it, and what do they get?**
Who actually touches this — you, your team, the public, other software? What does each of them get out of it?

**What are the rules this project lives by?**
Any hard lines or principles? Things like "we never throw away the raw data," "this has to keep running for years," "it must stay simple enough that one person can maintain it." The non-negotiables.

**What is this explicitly NOT?**
What's tempting to add but should stay out? Where's the edge? What would make you say "no, that's a different project"? (This one matters a lot — it's what stops the project from sprawling.)

**What would make this a success — or a failure?**
A year from now, how would you know it worked? And what would "it went wrong" look like?

**Anything else nagging at you.**
Constraints, history, a past attempt that failed, something you're worried about, a decision you've already made and don't want re-litigated. Say it all.
